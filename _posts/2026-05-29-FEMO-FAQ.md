I recently was fortunate to receive a "Backbone" scholarship from the [American Wildfire Experience](https://wildfire-experience.org/backbone-scholarship) and Mystery Ranch for the following submission to their biannual essay contest.

I wrote this piece as a resource for folks who are new to fire and might not know about these kind of jobs. I'm posting it here in addition to the Mystery Ranch website in hopes that someone just getting into fire reads it and gets excited about the important work wildland fire modules and FEMOs do. 

One of the cool things I discovered about wildland firefighting that I didn't appreciate until I was in it is that there are so many different flavors of firefighter. Whether you want to rappel out of a helicopter (crazy!), max out your masochism on a hotshot crew, nerd out over science (me), or just sit in a truck all day (sorry engine slugs <3), there's a place for you somewhere. You just have to know what to look for!

Big thank you to my friend and mentor Matt Biggs, who first told me about wildland fire modules and fire effects crews back when I was in AmeriCorps. Wanted to pay his kindness forward :).
